---
title: Pre-Club Checklist
summary: Everything to pack, print, know, and rehearse before your first club night — so tonight is about chess, not logistics.
---

Club night is the destination this whole manual points at. The night itself
is mostly out of your control — pairings, opposition, the room's noise. What
you *can* control is everything up to the pairing call, and a checklist does
that work better than nerves or bravery. Print this page, tick it off, go.

## Know-before-you-go (call or check the club's page)

- [ ] Night, time, and venue confirmed — including parking or the bus, and
      whether the entrance looks different after dark
- [ ] Format: quick tournament, long swiss, casual ladders — and whether
      there's a **beginners' or lower section** (ask; there usually is)
- [ ] Typical time control: often around 30 or 60 minutes per side, with or
      without an increment (chapter 6 covers the phase budgets)
- [ ] Whether scoresheets are provided or bring-your-own (the companion
      scoresheet pack's prints work for both)
- [ ] Cost: entry fee / membership day rate (usually modest; usually cash or
      a simple transfer)
- [ ] Organizer's name, so you can say "I spoke to ___" in your head

## Gear — one small bag

- [ ] 2 pencils (they break, they vanish) — **never pens**
- [ ] A scoresheet or two, plus the post-game reflection page
- [ ] The margin cheat-card from chapter 4 (the 30-second version)
- [ ] Water; a snack for between rounds
- [ ] Any glasses you actually use; the room lighting varies
- [ ] Phone silenced and in the bag before the first round

## Knowledge gate (chapter 2 has you covered)

- [ ] Castling conditions — including the king's two-square path rule
- [ ] En passant — you've seen it happen once, on purpose
- [ ] Touch move, and "j'adoube" for adjusting
- [ ] Stalemate = draw, not a win for whoever's ahead
- [ ] Notation: you can write `Nf3`, `exd5`, `O-O` without hesitation

## The arrival script (chapter 8 rehearsed this)

- [ ] Arrive 15 minutes early
- [ ] Say the words: "Hi — I'm a new player, first night tonight. What
      should I know before I get paired?" (the organizer will take it
      from there; this one sentence does most of the social work of the
      evening)
- [ ] Handshake before and after every game, win or lose
- [ ] Phones away before round one, not during it

## At the board, one-time reminders

- [ ] Press the clock with the same hand that moved the piece
- [ ] Move → press clock → write the move (chapter 5's order)
- [ ] C.C.T. on their move *first* (chapter 4)
- [ ] Resignation on the board: tip your king or lay it down politely, offer
      the handshake, and say "good game"
- [ ] When in doubt about anything procedural, ask the arbiter or the
      organizer — that's their whole job at a club level

## Homecoming (30 gentle minutes, maximum)

- [ ] Annotate tonight's game: the three questions from chapter 5
- [ ] Write the week's "one thing" on the training-card system you already
      keep (or the scoresheet pack's lesson-notes page)
- [ ] Mark the next club night in the calendar **now**, while the tea is
      still warm — commitment is cheapest at peak enthusiasm

After the first night, this checklist retires most of itself. By the third
visit you'll pack by muscle memory and arrive at the pairing table already
there. That's the real sign you've become a club player — the logistics
stopped being the event.
