You're not starting from zero. You know how the pieces move, which is genuinely
the hardest part to explain to anyone. What you're missing isn't talent — it's
the dusty layer of habits, etiquette, and quiet confidence that people pick up
by hanging around clubs. Most adults skip the club years and expect to catch up
alone, in secret, on their phones, feeling slightly embarrassed the whole time.

This manual is the friendly shortcut through that layer. It's a **comeback
guide** in two senses: a return if you played years ago (school club, grandpa's
kitchen table, a 2003 copy of Chessmaster), and the first lap if this is really
new. Either way, the path is the same:

1. **You can already do** — reading this, setting a board up once a week, and
   finishing games. That's most of the battle, and it's yours.
2. **This guide adds** — opening *principles* (not memorized opening books),
   a blunder-recovery ritual you can feel in your shoulders, how to annotate
   your own games, how to spend clock time in each phase, real-world etiquette,
   the pre-club night checklist, and gentle training weeks that fit around
   groceries, shifts, kids, and everything else adult life is.

## What this is not

- **Not a course.** No homework deadlines, no 1,000-puzzle gauntlet. Every
  chapter works on its own.
- **Not opening theory.** You will learn *zero* memorized opening lines here,
  on purpose (see [Opening Principles](03-opening-principles.md)).
- **Not advice for experts.** When a choice comes up, this guide always picks
  the friendlier-for-adults option: shorter games first, fewer things to track,
  and permission to table the rest.

## The one promise this makes

Somewhere in the next few months there is a moment where you sit across from a
stranger, shake hands, write "1. e4" on a real scoresheet, and later — however
the game goes — review it over a coffee without the dread. That moment is the
whole destination, and this guide is built to get you there without burning
out on the way.
